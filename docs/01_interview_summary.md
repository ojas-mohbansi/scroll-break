# Anonymized User Interview Summary

## Overview & Objectives
To establish a human-centered foundation for **ScrollBrake**, semi-structured user interviews were conducted with 4 potential users (university students and young working professionals aged 19–27). The goal was to understand their daily digital wellness habits, identify specific difficulties related to smartphone screen time, evaluate existing tools/alternatives, and pinpoint unaddressed needs.

---

## Interviewee Profiles

### Participant A (P1)
- **Role:** Undergraduate Student (Computer Science, Age 21)
- **Primary Apps:** Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube
- **Average Screen Time:** 5.5 hours/day
- **Current Habits:** Scrolls reels in bed before sleep and during short breaks between study sessions.
- **Pain Point:** "I open Instagram to check a message, see a reel, and suddenly 45 minutes have passed. Standard app timers are useless because I just tap 'Ignore limit for today'."

### Participant B (P2)
- **Role:** Graduate Student & Researcher (Biology, Age 24)
- **Primary Apps:** Twitter/X, Reddit, Instagram
- **Average Screen Time:** 4.8 hours/day
- **Current Habits:** Doomscrolls news and research feeds while working on papers.
- **Pain Point:** "Hard blockers feel too punitive. When an app completely locks me out when I need it for a quick break, I end up turning off the blocker entirely."

### Participant C (P3)
- **Role:** Junior Software Engineer (Age 23)
- **Primary Apps:** TikTok, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn
- **Average Screen Time:** 6.2 hours/day
- **Current Habits:** Scrolls short-form content absentmindedly during work-from-home focus hours.
- **Pain Point:** "I don't realize I'm mindlessly scrolling until I've been doing it fast for 20 minutes. I need a gentle speed bump or nudge, not a brick wall."

### Participant D (P4)
- **Role:** Marketing Specialist (Age 26)
- **Primary Apps:** Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest
- **Average Screen Time:** 5.0 hours/day
- **Current Habits:** High scroll velocity, rapid context switching across visual social feeds.
- **Pain Point:** "Digital wellbeing dashboards make me feel guilty with dark red graphs showing how many hours I wasted, but they don't give me positive motivation or tools to interrupt the urge in the moment."

---

## Key Themes & Findings

1. **The "Bypass Reflex" in Existing Tools:**
   - All 4 participants actively use built-in tools (Apple Screen Time or Google Digital Wellbeing).
   - All participants reported disabling or bypassing hard time-limit restrictions within seconds.

2. **Rapid Mindless Scroll Velocity vs. Conscious Intent:**
   - Unconscious "doomscrolling" is characterized by rapid, repetitive swipe gestures.
   - Users express a desire for an **interruption mechanism (friction speed bump)** rather than permanent lockouts.

3. **Guilt vs. Positive Reinforcement:**
   - Traditional screen-time dashboards rely on shame/guilt metrics ("You spent 4h 12m today").
   - Users respond significantly better to positive habit tracking, intention setting, and streak-based reflection.

4. **Contextual Intention Setting:**
   - Users want to state *why* they are opening an app (e.g., "Checking a specific message for 5 minutes") so they can hold themselves accountable.

## Anonymization & Privacy Compliance
All participant names and identifying information have been anonymized. No personal usage data, private messages, or browsing histories were collected or logged during these interviews.
